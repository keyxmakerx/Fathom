The edge vocabulary
the lack of types of lines — answered
Thesis
what changed, what it cost

In one sentence: the five rejected drawings failed because every mark in them was a 1px --ink line on a --page ground with no layout behind it — this one runs a real layered layout and a real orthogonal router, spends the two unspent neutral channels (fill and weight) on what a thing is rather than on hover, and draws the eleven-form edge vocabulary that 56 §5.2 already owned and no concept had ever drawn.

What I changed, against the rejected round

  • Two weights at exactly 2:1. Device and peer boundaries are 2px --ink; every edge, bracket, band, stub, tick, chassis rule and annotation is 1px. In all five concepts a device boundary and a port stub were the same mark.
  • A three-step tonal ladder. Canvas --surface-2, site region --surface, device box --page. The concepts drew --page boxes on a --page ground — an outline floating on nothing — and spent fill on hover instead.
  • Selection surrenders the weight channel. Selection is ▸ on the label, a --surface ground, and everything outside the closure at 40% opacity. Weight now means structure, permanently.
  • Layout is computed. Semantic rank table, median crossing reduction with a fixed sweep count, a priority-method x-assignment, per-corridor interval-graph channel allocation, corridor centring, bend-first route ordering. Nothing is a literal. Drag a box and it pins; the rest flows around it.
  • Semantic sides. Uplinks leave the top, downlinks the bottom, management the left, WAN and tunnels the right — regardless of slot. This is the single largest legibility win in the file and it costs no channel.
  • Terminals are explicit geometry. 8px perpendicular port stub on the 4px edge lattice, then 4px of clearance before the first bend. Nothing lands on a wall at an arbitrary slope; there is not one diagonal in the picture.
  • Crossings and junctions are different pictures. A 4px knockout gap in the lower-priority rail at every genuine crossing; a 4px filled --ink square at every genuine tee. In the rejected round a four-way meeting and a connection were the same plus sign.
  • Quantised widths, fixed heights, one gutter. Widths on a {96,128,160,192} ladder, heights 36/52 plus 20px chassis sub-rows, column gap raised from 32px to 64px so a rank reads as objects rather than as one stripe.
  • Two type sizes, not three. --t-mono for identifiers, --t-micro for annotation. The third level is bought with case and tracking. --t-tab does not appear in the picture.
  • Crispness. vector-effect="non-scaling-stroke" on every stroked element, shape-rendering="crispEdges" on boxes, rails and bands, positions on the 4px lattice and 1px strokes emitted at +0.5. Four of the five concepts had no vector-effect at all and stretched a fixed viewBox to 100% width, so every hairline was a half-intensity smear across two device rows.

The colour question, answered honestly

Monochrome is not the problem and I would not reopen ADR-0011. Six of Bertin's seven retinal variables survive the ban; only hue is lost. The rejected concepts left three of the remaining six completely unused — value, texture and size — and then complained about having five. ISO 128-2 separates five line meanings on weight and dash alone; USPTO figures encode material type in hatch on nothing but black ink. What this picture was starving for was fill and weight, both neutral, both already tokenised, both unspent.

Hatch is deliberately not used here. 51 §4.1 C2 already spends hatch twice at 135° for suppressed findings and the AI-proposed gutter; if the diagram ever takes it, it must be 45°, must mean exactly "beyond this boundary is not modelled", and must be stroked geometry rather than a paint server, because <pattern> is outside 34 §5.6's tag set and a paint server dies under forced-colors.

Which disclosure mechanism I would ship

Ship the inspector column as the mechanism. Ship the ▸ depth mark before either. Ship the chip only as a preview, bounded by a content rule.

The owner's question underneath "do they each have popups" is is there more here? — and a hover cannot answer it, because you have to already be pointing at the thing to find out. The static ▸ at a node's bottom-right answers it at a glance, at every zoom, in print, on touch, in the exported SVG, and for a screen-reader user. It costs no new glyph and no interaction model, because ▸ already means "disclosable, there is more" in four other places in the product.

Flip the toggle above and feel the two. The chip here is fully WCAG 1.4.13 compliant — dismissible with Esc without moving the pointer, hoverable with a grace corridor, persistent with no auto-hide timer, triggered by :focus as well as :hover, and drawn with a 1px --ink rule and no shadow. It is buildable and it is compliant. It is still the weaker mechanism, for three reasons that are not about accessibility: it occludes exactly the neighbours you are comparing against and it does so worst where density is highest; every fact that lives only in a chip is lost by the SVG, the PNG, the print, the screenshot and the ticket; and 56 §9.5 already prints the hidden-label table beneath the picture because a printed page has no hover.

So the chip is bound by a content rule that keeps it honest: it may contain only what the picture already tried to draw and could not — a truncated name, a label the placer counted rather than placed, an age line the zoom suppressed, a port name the LOD ladder dropped. Four lines maximum. If the picture drew it, the chip does not repeat it. Under that rule the chip is a label that would not fit, not an inspector in disguise, and 51 §11's refusal of inspector-as-overlay is left intact.

What I sacrificed

  • Brandes–Köpf. x-assignment is the priority method with a fixed pass count, not the four-alignment median. It is deterministic and it is good enough at this size; it will produce longer edges than BK on a wide graph.
  • Label placement is fixed-side first. The E/W/N/S search runs only on collision, and demotions are counted in the view band next to hidden labels (n moved). That is deliberate — inconsistent label sides are, after staircase routing, the most reliable tell that nobody drew this.
  • The single scene-level knockout path. Rule 14 asks for one <path> of knockout subpaths painted between the bands and the edges. That cannot express per-pair priority — a knockout painted under every edge knocks out nothing, and painted over every edge it erases the winner too. I used containerlab's actual mechanism instead: the gap is cut into the lower-priority path's own geometry. It costs zero elements rather than one and it is exact.
  • The 4px gap between the stub end and the rail. Refused, and this one is load-bearing. A 4px break in a 1px monochrome line is not distinguishable from --rule-style-pending, and putting one at both ends of every edge in the picture would have re-created the exact defect the dash reservation exists to prevent. The 4px is kept as bend clearance instead: an edge travels 12px perpendicular before it may turn, so no corner ever touches a wall.
  • Sub-pixel truth below 1:1. The +0.5 stroke offset is exact at z = 1 and z = 2 only. Below that, non-scaling-stroke keeps the stroke 1 CSS px but cannot control which device row it lands on.

Three defects in the spec that this build found

1. --dg-conduit-gap: 5px and --dg-rail-gap: 3px are off the 4px lattice that 56 §5.3 declares two lines above them. They are also too small to survive: with non-scaling-stroke a 5px scene gap between two 1px rails is 2.5 screen px at z = 0.5 and 1.75px at the 0.35 LOD floor, at which point the conduit and the twin rail both degenerate into the single undifferentiated line the owner complained about. This build uses 8px for the conduit and 4px for the rail pair, both on the lattice, and enforces the minimum separation in screen pixels — below it the form degrades to a named fallback and the view band says so.

2. 56 §5.6's width formula guarantees a ragged rank. SRX-A is 64px and SRX-345-DC-EAST is 140px, so a row of devices is a row of unequal boxes. Quantising to a 32px ladder costs one ceil and preserves §2.4's arithmetic determinism. The ladder starts at 96, not 64, because at 64 a device carrying chassis sub-rows cannot hold its own sub-row labels.

3. A device's height has to be allowed to grow with its port count. The hub in this fixture terminates one tunnel per spoke; at 40 spokes its right edge carries 40 st0 units. Holding the box at 36px and drawing 40 stubs on it is the texture 56 §12 predicts. Letting the box grow turns it into a 40-pin symbol with its unit names set inside the body, which is what schematic capture has done for forty years and is the most legible thing on the page at 40 spokes.

Measured, not asserted

Chromium, file://, DPR 2. Every number below is printed live in the view band as you change the estate size.

4 spokes — 13 nodes, 18 edges, 187 elements, scene 784 × 668, 2 routing channels, 5 crossings, layout 3.1 ms, DOM build 4.6 ms, fit 0.98.
12 spokes — 21 nodes, 26 edges, 259 elements, scene 832 × 796, 6 channels, 5 crossings, layout 5–9 ms, DOM build 13 ms, fit 0.93.
40 spokes — 49 nodes, 54 edges, 510 elements, scene 1000 × 1332, 20 channels, 5 crossings, layout 13 ms, DOM build 17 ms, fit 0.68.

Zero edges cross a device box at any of the three. Zero routing segments are diagonal — the only four non-axis-aligned segments in the file are the two open-V arrowhead legs, which are terminal glyphs on the 4px lattice, not routes. Every stroked element carries vector-effect="non-scaling-stroke". The picture resolves to exactly six values, all neutral: --ink, --muted, --hairline, --surface, --surface-2, --page. No risk colour appears in the SVG at all.

Where this does not yet hold up

Aspect ratio is the real weak point. The peer column is a single stack, so from 4 to 40 spokes the scene gets 1.28× wider and 2× taller. The pane grows with it, capped at 900px, which is the only reason the 40-spoke fit lands at 0.68 rather than 0.48 — in a fixed 640px pane the estate would cross the 0.6 threshold and lose its edge labels and port names. That is announced rather than hidden, but it is degradation. The two fixes, in order of preference, are 56 §12's own recommendation — collapse st0.* into one stub with a count and expand on drill-down — and wrapping the peer stack into a second column, which needs a half-pitch stagger so the second column's conduits can reach it through the first column's gutters. Neither is built here.

Three smaller honest failures. (i) 8 port labels out of roughly 40 cannot be placed at 4 spokes and are counted in the view band rather than overlapped — that is the mechanism working, but 8 is more than it should be and the fix is a fifth candidate position rather than a cleverer search. (ii) A rank with two disconnected halves cannot spread, because the rigid re-centring step that kills the drift also caps how far a rank can pull apart; the left third of the site region is empty for that reason and 56 §3.6 predicts exactly this cost. (iii) Multi-rank edges run in a margin lane instead of through phase 4's dummy nodes. It is the scoped answer and it is what a draughtsman does with a signal that crosses a whole sheet, but it is not the textbook one and it will not scale past a handful of such edges.
