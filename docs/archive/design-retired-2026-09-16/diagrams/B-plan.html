Thesis

what I did · what it cost

The flatness was tone and weight, not hue. The five rejected concepts drew a device boundary, a link, a zone bracket and a port stub with the identical 1 px stroke, filled every box --page on a --page ground, and spent the only channel they did use — fill — on hover. This page changes three things and nothing else: two stroke weights at exactly 2:1, a three-step neutral tonal ladder, and an edge vocabulary that is actually drawn. There is no colour in the picture at any zoom, in either theme — verified by an audit that walks every painted element and compares its resolved stroke and fill against the six neutral tokens. Do not reopen ADR-0011. It was never the problem.

What changed from the rejected round

  • Layout is computed. Ten phases run in the page: project, semantic rank, group, order (fixed 4-sweep median), constrained placement with containment, container geometry, semantic port pinning, routing, edge events, labels. There is not one hard-coded x or y in the scene. Grow the estate and watch it recompute.
  • Two weights, 2:1. Device and peer boundaries at --dg-stroke-struct (2 px); every edge, bracket, band, stub, tick and rule at 1 px. ISO 128's proportion, which Fathom already owned and never used.
  • Three tonal fills. Canvas --surface-2, region wash --surface, boxes --page. Fill encodes what a thing is, permanently. Selection does not touch the weight channel at all.
  • Eleven edge types, all drawn. Plain link, LAG, reth, conduit, routed adjacency, policy direction, access, trunk, asymmetric trunk, inferred, AI-proposed — plus two edge events, the crossing break and the junction square. No dash and no dot carries a topology meaning: G2 is spent product-wide on AI-proposed.
  • Brackets are structure, not annotation. The three zone brackets form a left margin column that reads before the boxes do. The VLAN band is horizontal, the routing instance is a closed box — G7's three forms are three different drawings.
  • Edges never touch anything. 8 px perpendicular port stub outside the box, 4 px gap to the rail, anchored on the 4 px lattice. Sides are semantic: uplinks leave the top, downlinks the bottom, WAN and tunnels the right.
  • The ▸ depth mark. Static, printable, zoom-independent, in the Outline — it answers "is there more here?" without requiring you to point at the thing first. It is drawn in both disclosure modes.

What I sacrificed

  • Node names truncate. Width is quantised to {96, 128, 160, 192}. A ragged row of boxes reads as generated; a quantised row reads as drawn. The cost is an ellipsis, and the full name is in the Outline row, the inspector and the chip.
  • The picture got wider. 64 px column gutters and a 60 px routing channel mean the scene needs more room than the rejected round's 10–22 px gutters. At 40 spokes it does not fit at zoom 1 and the view drops to fit-zoom, which turns edge labels off. That is announced, not hidden.
  • Conduits aggregate above eight spokes. Forty individually-drawn conduits into one device is a texture, which is 56 §12's own open decision. The view band says conduits aggregated and the control lets you force every one and see for yourself.
  • The transit-map idiom, dropped on a measurement. This attempt was commissioned to test octilinear (45°-permitted) routing. It is built and it is one click away. It lost, and not on taste — see below.

The one experiment that failed, with the number

Rendered in Chromium at device pixel ratio 1, sampled straight off the page:

  • An axis-aligned 1 px --ink rail covers exactly one device pixel at channel value 20 — full --ink, no antialiasing at all. The half-pixel stroke offset, shape-rendering="crispEdges" and vector-effect="non-scaling-stroke" together do their job perfectly.
  • A 45° 1 px --ink rail covers one device pixel at channel value 92.
  • An axis-aligned 1 px --muted rail covers one device pixel at channel value 92.

Those last two numbers are the same number, and it is not a coincidence: --muted is #5C6772, whose red channel is literally 92. Against the --surface region ground the axis-aligned ink rail measures 16.5:1 and the diagonal measures 6.0:1 — it loses 64% of its contrast and lands exactly on the tone the channel budget has already spent.

So a 45° run of a deterministic edge is, on screen, the same colour as an inferred edge. That is not an aesthetic quibble; it silently collides with G1 (boundary tone = fresh versus stale) and with the edge-tone channel (observed versus inferred). Transit maps get away with 45° because their lines are twenty units wide. Fathom's ladder is 1 px and 2 px, tone is load-bearing, and at that weight the diagonal is not available. Orthogonal is the default here and octilinear is the toggle, with the reason printed in the control band rather than kept in a document.

Which disclosure I would ship

The inspector column, with the ▸ mark built first and the chip as an optional preview. Both are built here and the toggle is live, so the difference is feelable rather than argued.

The chip satisfies WCAG 1.4.13 in full — dismissible with Escape without moving the pointer, hoverable, persistent with no timer, and triggered by focus as well as hover, with a 1 px --ink rule and no shadow. It is buildable and compliant. It is still the wrong mechanism, for three reasons the toggle makes visible: it occludes exactly the neighbours you are comparing against, and it does so worst where density is highest; every fact that lives only in a chip is lost by the SVG export, the print, the screenshot and the ticket, and the diagram is the most-circulated artifact the product makes; and 56 §9.5 already prints the hidden-label table beneath the picture because a printed page has no hover, which a chip-borne fact would falsify.

So the chip is bound by a content rule that keeps it honest: it may contain only what the picture already tried to draw and could not — a truncated name, a label the placer counted instead of placing, an age line the zoom suppressed, a port name the LOD ladder dropped. Four lines maximum. The last line is always ↵ inspector. Under that rule it is a label that would not fit, not an inspector in disguise. Turn the estate to 40 and switch modes: in column mode the picture stays readable while you work; in chip mode you are reading through a hole you are holding.

Fixture. The estate below is a fixture derived from .context/field-card-srx-ipsec.txt — the owner's own four-side SRX IPsec card. SITE-A terminates IKE on reth0.0, which is why SRX-A is a two-chassis cluster; the remote peers are the card's 203.0.113.x worked example, multiplied. Command text and explainers in the inspector are quoted from corpus/commands/junos-srx-ipsec.yaml and carry that file's own warning: reviewed_by: <named human> is a placeholder and nothing in it has been run on a box. No device was contacted to draw any of this and none ever will be.

Measured, in this page, live. The figures in the view band and the scene footer are computed by the code that draws the picture — layout milliseconds, element count, edges demoted from diagonal to orthogonal because a diagonal would have crossed a box, labels hidden, moved, plated, crossings broken, junctions drawn, and the on-screen rail and conduit separations in CSS pixels at the current zoom. Nothing there is typed.

Two decisions this page takes, that the corpus leaves open

▸ means "there is more here", not "selected". Concepts 01 and 05 both spent ▸ on the selected node's label, which collides with all four of its existing product-wide uses. Here it keeps the product-wide meaning and sits at the bottom-right of any element whose inspector holds more than the picture shows. Selection is instead the --surface ground on the node plus everything outside the closure at 40% — so selection surrenders the stroke-weight channel entirely and 2 px goes to structure, permanently.

Fill is a channel and selection pays for it. The budget goes from ten to eleven and the payment is that selection stops being an element-property channel at all. Selection is a property of the view, not of the element. That is an argument, not a rule, and the owner should rule on it.

What did not work, stated plainly

  • Octilinear routing. Commissioned, built, measured, dropped. Numbers above.
  • Drawing forty conduits individually. Switch conduits to draw every one at 40 spokes and see it: the peer grid has to collapse to a single column (a conduit must reach its own peer's left edge and a wrapped grid puts three columns of boxes in the way), the scene grows to 2,308 px tall, 510 crossings are broken, 44 labels cannot be placed and one route still cannot clear a box. That picture is the argument for aggregating, and it is why the shipped default aggregates above eight and says so in the view band.
  • Three node widths, not one. The ladder resolves to 96 / 128 / 160 px here, because width is driven by the age line in --t-micro rather than by the name. Regular, but not the single repeated module a rack elevation gets. Shortening the age register to 4 months from parsed 4 months ago would collapse it to one width; that is 56 §8.1's wording to change, not this page's.
  • Three labels the placer could not place at the default estate — ge-0/0/1, ge-0/0/2 and next-hop. They are counted in the view band and they are in the Outline and the inspector. Counting is the only honest option; overlapping them is not.
