Following a Flow

Five ways to track a packet across devices, racks and buildings without leaving the canvas. Each was designed against the real schema, then torn into by someone who did not write it.

You asked to follow a TCP flow to another location, cross between racks without losing the thread, and descend into a box to see why it goes where it goes. Five directions below. The drawings are in the product’s own palette — real hex values, square corners, no shadows — so you are judging the actual look.

What every direction agreed on

Your trace is already specified, and nobody has written it. The corpus defines trace_step in full — the cable walk, the sort orders, the patch-panel pass-through, a 16-hop cap, and seven named outcomes including ambiguous and horizon. It has never been implemented. The design work here is mostly about how to draw something that was decided long ago.

But nothing can create a cable. Cable, Terminates and PassThrough are all declared — and no operation builds one. So the physical trace is fully expressible and completely unbuildable on a hand-made estate. An OP_CABLE is the prerequisite for the first two of your three asks, and it is not optional in any of these five.

And you can answer “why here?” honestly without a rules engine. Four of the five landed on the same move independently: never say permitted or denied. Instead, from the interface the traffic came in on and the one it leaves by, name the two zones, the policy set between them, and that set’s policies in the order the device reads them. Four hundred policies on the box become the twenty-seven pointing this direction — exactly, not by guesswork. It is a narrowing rather than a verdict, and it is buildable now.

The Open Spine

the path stays open, everything else folds

viableno engine needed

Pick a path and the estate folds around it — the two racks it crosses stand open, everything else closes to a box, and zoom decides how far along the thread you spend detail rather than which screen you are on.

Crossing to another rack

There is no crossing, because there is no jumping. Both racks are on the same canvas at the same time. The thread leaves rack R12's open elevation through the frame edge and enters rack R04's open elevation, and it is the same unbroken stroke — the thing requirement 2 asks you not to lose is never let go of.

Why it goes here

A gutter under the canvas, opened with `w`, keyed to the focus segment's device. It is built entirely by graph walking, and it is careful about which question it is answering.

Hundreds of things

Four reductions, each with a named filter, a named ranking and a stated default. The worked numbers are for an SRX with 6 zones, 220 policies and 341 static routes — a shape the owner's work estate plausibly has.

The argument against

The strongest argument against is aspect ratio, and it is not fixable by cleverness. An open 42U elevation is one box wide and forty units tall. Two of them side by side with a faceplate between makes a canvas roughly 900 units wide and 3,000 tall — a shape no screen has. The gradient caps the COUNT of open things at three, which is what makes the direction survivable, but it cannot cap their HEIGHT. In practice the user scrolls vertically to follow a thread that reads horizontally, which is the worst of both axes, and an owner documenting a home lab with two 24U racks will hit it on his first real path. The mitigations are honest but partial: draw only the occupied U-range plus two units of context and print `U1–U13 elided`, and auto-fit the viewport to the thread's bounding box rather than the estate's. Neither makes a 42U rack short.

The Run Sheet

the trace is a document the canvas illustrates

viablepartly needs an engine

A trace is a numbered list of hops you can read, filter and paste into a ticket; the canvas is the illustration of whichever hop your cursor is standing on.

Crossing to another rack

A rack or premises change is a STANZA RULE drawn across the document — a hairline, the CLLI, the premises label, the rack label, and the word `jump`. It is inserted between hop N and hop N+1 whenever the premises of the hop's chassis (via `HasChassis` → `MountedIn` → `Rack` → `HasRack` → `Premises`) differs from the previous hop's. Computed once when the sheet is built, from asserted containment only, therefore deterministic.

Why it goes here

Requirement 3 is the because rows, and the design's load-bearing refusal is that they CITE rather than DECIDE.

Hundreds of things

Five named mechanisms, and four of the five already exist in the product.

The argument against

HE ASKED FOR A CANVAS AND THIS ANSWERS WITH A LIST. Verbatim: "the goal is I should be able to track a tcp all the way to a different location VIA THE CANVAS VIEWER." Direction 4 demotes the canvas to a passenger — it illustrates, it never leads — and that is not what the man said. Worse, it is plausibly the direction that gets built precisely BECAUSE it is cheapest, which means the cost argument could win a fight the product argument should lose. If it does, the owner receives a run sheet where he asked for a map, and a run sheet is uncomfortably close to what he can already assemble from `show route`, `show security flow session` and a spreadsheet. The reason to build this tool rather than keep the spreadsheet is that it DRAWS. A direction that makes drawing optional has quietly conceded the differentiator, and the four zoom rungs he has already seen and accepted end up with much less to do — the rack elevation he asked for by name becomes a thing you glance at rather than a place you work.

Facing Pages

both ends on screen at once

check incompletenot checked

Two panes with the whole path running down the gutter between them: you always see two hops at full detail and every hop as a row on the spine, so crossing into another rack or another building isn't a jump — the far side is already the pane on the right.

Crossing to another rack

There is no jump, and that is the point. In a one-pane design, crossing a rack boundary means replacing what is on screen and hoping the user remembers what they were on. Facing Pages never replaces anything: the far side of the crossing is the right pane, already drawn, already at whatever depth makes it legible.

Why it goes here

This is the requirement most likely to be faked, so here is exactly what is shown and exactly what is refused.
