Concept 03 — canvas-first · thesis, costs and the seam

The thesis, one sentence

One scene, four questions: the estate is drawn once, the picture never moves, and config, findings, diagram and inventory stop being four places you go and become four annotations of the same geometry — so switching view is switching question, and the picture is the index into every other surface.

What changed from the starting posture, and why

The posture was "the diagram is the application". Taken literally that produces floating panels over an infinite canvas, and the visual law forbids all three of the things that makes workable — elevation, tooltips and overlays (51 §11). It also fails the product's own governing rule: a picture that owns the panels starts owning the state. So the canvas here is not a workspace, it is the view body of the sheet. The furniture above it is unchanged from 52 §2.2, the second surface is a column at 62/38, and every gesture on the picture terminates in an op against the graph, printed in the op ledger under the scene so you can see it did. The divergent move is not the canvas — it is that the canvas persists across the view switch and the other views render as annotation plus a docked table.

What it optimises for — and what it therefore sacrifices

  • Optimises: spatial recall. The scene coordinates are stable across alt 3–alt 6, so "the WAN unit is the second box on the right" stays true in the findings view, the config view and the inventory view. Selection is the through-line and you can watch it survive four renderings.
  • Sacrifices, first: vertical space. The scene costs roughly 300px of body height permanently, on top of the ~210px furniture budget. On a 1280×800 laptop that leaves under 300px for the docked table — about eight findings rows. A list-first direction gets twenty.
  • Sacrifices, second: the picture cannot draw everything, and this concept refuses to pretend otherwise. IkeProposal, IkePolicy, IpsecProposal and IpsecPolicy have no rendering in 56 §4.1's projection table at any layer. Six of the nineteen emitted lines, five of the twenty inventory rows and one of the three findings belong to nodes that are not in the picture at all — including ipsec.pfs.absent, which is the highest-value security finding on the card. They are marked not drawn · inspector only wherever they appear, and selecting one puts not in diagram in the selection ribbon. A canvas-first tool has to say that out loud on every screen, and it is the single biggest argument against the direction.
  • Sacrifices, third: below 1100px the second surface is unavailable (52 §2.3), and a canvas plus a docked table plus no inspector is a worse single-column experience than a list-first direction would have. The refusal states its reason in the footer rather than disabling anything.

How it serves teaching — goal (a)

Teaching is attached to whatever you pointed at. Select reth0.0 and the inspector carries the corpus entry for junos-srx/ike.gateway.external-interface.set at three depths — terse, explained, teaching — with the depth control as a lowercase margin tab on the block (v or alt \), never a setting. The three depths are three texts, not three lengths, and they are verbatim from the corpus. Every explainer header carries the ADR-0027 verification stamp, which for this corpus reads junos-srx · unverified, because the seed file's own header says nothing in it has been run on a box. The card's teaching devices survive: next_if_bad renders as a ladder with a risk word per rung, sources cite the card side and block, and output_fields render as the card's two-column hairline table. The finder (Ctrl+K) is the corpus surface and works with no workspace at all — it is the one view whose primary object is a CorpusId, so it is also the one view that does not annotate the scene.

How it serves inventory and diagramming — goal (b)

alt 6 docks the estate table under the same scene. Columns are generated from the schema, the rightmost column is the per-row finding aggregate as text, and sorting by it sorts by worst severity then count. Below it, in the note idiom, are the population rules — the inventory's opinions. TWO SRX DEVICES, NO CLUSTER fires on this fixture and argues back; [ model it as a cluster ] writes a real node and appends the op, and [ not a cluster ] suppresses the observation and counts it in the band. Management by direct manipulation is real: drag a box, or use ←→ (4px) and shift ←→ (32px), and one Op::SetLayoutHint is appended per gesture. Try to move a zone bracket or a conduit and it refuses in the footer, with the reason — those are derived renderings, not positioned nodes. Layout hints are state; topology is not.

Forty spokes on one hub — the honest answer

Press + 8 spokes. Below the element ceiling the spokes are drawn. Above it the view aggregates to one node and hands off — "an engineer who wants their whole estate on one screen cannot get it, and the answer is the inventory view" (52 §3.6.1). The ceiling counter under the scene is measured from the live DOM, not asserted. The product's ceiling is 2,000 live SVG elements; this fixture's is set low so you can cross it in two clicks. When it crosses, the population rule about clearing Phase 1 on a hub appears — forty spokes is forty tunnels renegotiating at once — which is the corpus teaching the scale problem it just caused.

Where new functionality plugs in — the seam, drawn on the page

The named seam is M7 — the schema is data, and it is drawn under the inventory table with the words this is where a new node kind plugs in. One row added to schema.yaml plus one row in 56 §4.1's projection table gives you, at once: new diagram geometry, new generated inventory columns, new rule targets and new explainer subjects, with no UI code — because the view contract is three pure functions (primary/resolve/select_at) and the core never knows a view exists. The second seam, also on the page and interactive, is M8 — population rules: the inventory's opinions are rules whose applies_to is a population, so the table acquires opinions it did not have yesterday without touching the table. Note what the seam is not: a seventh view. New functionality lands as a mode of an existing view, a layer that opens inside all of them, a new diagram layer, a new inventory kind or a new rule pack — verify(diff(graph)) already landed as config · change set, a mode, and explain(node, depth) already landed as a layer with three placements and no home.

How you can tell it cannot phone home

The margin tab no sockets, ever in the masthead opens the explainer layer in its sheet placement, subject THE ARTIFACT. It is not a badge and it is not green — green means read-only, and reusing a reserved colour for "offline" would be the most visible failure this concept could make. It is the card's two-column lookup table, and it audits this document live: it reads this page's own CSP out of the head, counts every element carrying an absolute URL, counts <script src> elements, and greps this page's own script text for call sites of the five network APIs. Every number is measured from the DOM in front of you, not asserted. The permanent version of the same fact sits in the footer: connect-src 'none' · 2 wasm imports — the WASM core's import section is two entries, which is the check that makes the header an architectural property rather than a promise.

The phase boundary, stated rather than resolved

ADR-0006 decides that v1 is the finder alone, with no graph. Both of the owner's stated goals need the graph. Everything in this concept that is corpus-backed today — the finder, the three-depth explainers, the authored ladders, the risk legend, the verification stamp — carries no phase tab. Everything graph-backed later — the scene, config, findings, inventory, the walkthrough — sits under the masthead tab graph-backed · phase 1–2. By 71 §2's own figures that is 38–54 solo weeks beyond v1 before this screen can exist at all, and 83 §12.5 found those inputs low by 1.5–1.6×. The concept is drawn so the owner can see exactly which half of it they would be buying.

The strongest argument against this direction

The picture is not a complete index, and a canvas-first tool implicitly promises that it is. Four of the six object-chain kinds on side 1 of the field card — the two proposals and the two policies — have no rendering at any layer, because they are sets of values, not places. So the most common real question ("why did Phase 2 fail?") points at IPSEC-POL, and IPSEC-POL is not on the canvas. This concept labels that everywhere rather than hiding it, but labelling a structural gap does not close it: a user who has learned to navigate by pointing will keep pointing at the nearest drawn thing and getting the wrong subject. A list-first or finder-first direction never has this problem, because a list can hold a row for anything with an ID.

Two smaller costs worth the owner's attention. The scene rent is permanent — it does not scale with how much you are getting from it, and on the findings view most users would rather have the rows. And a canvas invites exactly the failure 56 §0 names in caps: the moment someone adds a label, a colour or a grouping that lives only in the picture, the picture has become an undocumented data model. This concept guards it with the op ledger and by refusing to move derived geometry, but the guard is a discipline, not a mechanism, and disciplines erode.
