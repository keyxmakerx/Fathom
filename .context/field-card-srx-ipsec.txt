does not m ean the tunnel is dead.

TH E  M TU  S TO R Y
M TU is the largest fram e an interface will pass —  standard Ethernet 1500.
IPsec wraps every packet in new headers, so the payload that still fits
shrinks. Send a full 1500 into a tunnel and it m ust fragm ent, and firewalls
along the way routinely drop fragm ents.
THE TELL-TALE SYMPTOM
Ping works. SSH connects. Then ls hangs, or a transfer stalls at 0% .
Sm all packets fit; full-size ones exceed the tunnel M TU and vanish.
Handshake fine, data stalls = M TU until proven otherw ise.
O V E R H E A D  B U D G E T
LAYER BYTES
New outer IP header 20
ESP header + SPI + seq 8
IV 8–16
Pad + trailer + next-header 2–255
ICV (integrity) 12–16
NAT-T UDP 4500, if present +8
Roughly 50–70+ bytes, cipher-dependent. From  1500 that leaves about 1430
—  which is why tunnel M TU is conventionally clam ped to 1400 for headroom .
TH R E E  FIX E S  —  K N O W  TH E  D IFFE R E N C E
1 · MSS CLAMP —  PREFERRED
Rewrites the TCP handshake so both ends agree to sm aller segm ents up
front. Surgical, prevents fragm entation before it starts, needs no ICM P.
TCP only —  does nothing for UDP.
2 · LOW ER THE TUNNEL MTU
Universal, covers UDP, but the box now fragm ents before encryption.
Costs CPU, and those fragm ents can still be dropped downstream .
3 · DF-BIT CLEAR —  THE VALVE
Clears DF in the outer header so the network m ay fragm ent the encrypted
packet rather than drop it. The rescue when you control neither endpoint
nor the far side. Costs reassem bly at the peer. copy  (default) copies the
inner DF; set  forces it on.
Rule of thum b: M SS = tunnel M TU − 40 (IP + TCP). 1400 M TU →  clam p
1360; m any shops use 1350 for m argin.
P M TU D  &  TH E  D F B IT
Path M TU Discovery lets endpoints learn the sm allest hop by sending DF-set
packets; an oversized one should trigger an ICM P fragm entation needed
(Type 3 Code 4) back to the sender.
It breaks constantly because that ICM P is filtered som ewhere. The sender
never learns to shrink, and big packets disappear in silence. This is exactly
why M SS clam ping exists —  it does not depend on ICM P getting through.
R E P LAY E R R O R S
Every ESP packet carries a sequence num ber and the receiver keeps a
sliding window. Duplicate or too-old packets are dropped and counted as
replay.
Alm ost always benign reordering: ECM P or per-packet load-balancing in the
underlay, or rekey overlap. A sm all static count is noise. A clim bing count
m eans real reordering or loss —  and lost DPD probes on that sam e path will
tear the tunnel down. That is how replay counters and flapping connect.
